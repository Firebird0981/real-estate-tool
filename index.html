FHA charges 1.75% upfront plus 0.55% a year; a 3–4 unit building must also pass FHA's self-sufficiency test (75% of all units' rent must cover the full payment). Conventional 2–4 unit loans allow 5% down with private mortgage insurance (the 0.5%–1% a year depends on credit). VA loans have no down payment and a funding fee (about 2.15% on first use). Where these numbers come from, and why a stress test is useful

Most landlords who lose money did not buy a bad deal on paper. They bought a deal with no room for error, then hit a vacancy, a tax bill and a repair in the same year. A stress test shows that room before you buy, how much cash to keep in reserve, and which single risk matters most.

Shock (mild / moderate / bad year)What backs it
Rent −4% / −8% / −15%Our rent model is typically 7.5–9.8% off where we can check it. In the last year, Zillow asking rents in 140 NJ ZIPs ranged from −1.2% (worst 5%) to +6.7% (best 10%). These cuts are harsher than anything seen last year, so they model a real downturn, not a forecast.
Vacancy +3 / +6 ptsU.S. Census (ACS): the median NJ ZIP has 2.1% of rentals empty and for rent, the 75th percentile 4.7%, the 90th percentile 8.2%. The bad year is about the gap to the 90th-percentile ZIP.
Rate +0.5 / +1 / +2 ptsFreddie Mac: the 30-year rate went from 3.11% (Dec 2021) to 6.42% (Dec 2022), up 3.3 points in one year. Our worst case is milder than that.
Property tax +5% / +10% (or your town's own 2025 increase, up to +20%)NJ's average residential bill rose 4.7% in 2025 ($10,095 to $10,570); 155 of 564 towns rose more than 5% and 40 more than 10%. Town level: we compared each town's general tax rate for 2024 and 2025 (NJ Division of Taxation). The median town moved +2.8%. Backtest: in towns with no revaluation, the rate change matched the reported bill change closely (Cinnaminson 3.5% vs 3.6%, Moorestown 2.1% vs 2.4%, Cedar Grove 2.8% vs 3.1%, Chesilhurst 19.4% vs 20%). How the scores work

Safety (0–100) = how a place ranks among NJ ZIPs on police-reported crime per 1,000 residents: 65% violent crime, 35% property crime. 50 is typical; 80 means safer than about 80% of NJ ZIPs. Based on each town's own NJ State Police reports for 2023 and 2024, smoothed toward the county for small towns.
Schools (1–10) = how a school district ranks among NJ districts: 70% test results (share of students meeting state reading and math standards), 15% chronic absence, 15% four-year graduation rate where the district has a high school. Two years of state data, 2023–24 and 2024–25. It describes the whole district, not one school, and public schools only.
Livability (0–100) = weighted mix of safety, schools, jobs/commute, amenities and affordability (price-to-income ratio). Weights adjustable on Compare.
Market signals: price-to-rent ratio (under 15× favors cash flow, over 21× is an appreciation market); rent burden = typical rent ÷ median household income (over 30% means a stretched tenant pool); Section 8 edge = HUD 3-bed Fair Market Rent ÷ typical 3-bed market rent.
Deal grade (A–F): rentals earn points for positive cash flow, cash-on-cash ≥ 8%, DSCR ≥ 1.25 and after-tax IRR ≥ 10%; flips for profit margin on ARV and the 70% rule.
Investment score = each place × property type is underwritten (rent, taxes, insurance, maintenance, vacancy, management, HOA, mortgage). Cash-on-cash, cap rate, total return (cash flow + appreciation + principal paydown), safety and livability are scaled 0–100 across all options, then weighted by your goal.
Property types (relative to the county's Zillow typical value and typical asking rent): single-family (3-bedroom house) ≈ 0.90× value, 1.3× rent (2 and 4 bedrooms differ); condo ≈ 0.7× value with HOA; 2–4 unit ≈ 1.25× value, 3.0× rent (three 2-bedroom units); 5+ unit ≈ 2.6× value, 5.7× rent (six units of mixed size); short-term rentals use a tourism factor (strongest at the Shore) and ~18% extra operating costs.
Selling costs: NJ realty transfer fee (seller-paid) uses the Division of Taxation's official rate schedule; since July 2025 the graduated fee on sales over $1M is paid by the seller at 1% up to $2M, rising to 3.5% above $3.5M.

NJ investor checklist

• Rent control: roughly 100 NJ municipalities have rent-control ordinances, concentrated in Hudson, Essex, Union, Passaic and Middlesex. Check before assuming rent growth.
• Lead-safe certification: NJ requires lead-based paint inspections of most pre-1978 rental units every three years or at tenant turnover, whichever comes first.
• Flood risk: Shore and river towns may require flood insurance — get a quote before you offer.
• Short-term rentals: many towns license, cap or ban stays under 30 days.
• Certificate of occupancy / rental registration: most towns require an inspection between tenants.
• Property-tax appeals: if an assessment runs high relative to value, appeals go to the county tax board.
